Many puzzle apps promise to "train your brain" or "boost your memory". We don't make those claims about PaneSlide, because the evidence doesn't support them for games like ours. This page explains what reputable sources *do* say, what they don't, and how we have tried to make PaneSlide a calm, healthy kind of break.

**PaneSlide is a game, not a treatment.** If you are worried about your mental health or your memory, please talk to a doctor or a qualified health professional.

## What the NHS says about learning and attention

The UK's National Health Service publishes a short guide called [5 steps to mental wellbeing](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/self-help/guides-tools-and-activities/five-steps-to-mental-wellbeing/). Two of its five steps are relevant to hobbies like puzzles:

- **Learn new skills.** The NHS says research shows learning new skills can improve mental wellbeing by boosting self-confidence and self-esteem, helping you build a sense of purpose, and helping you connect with others. Among its suggestions is trying new hobbies that challenge you.
- **Pay attention to the present moment.** The NHS describes this as mindfulness, and says paying more attention to the present moment, including your thoughts, feelings and the world around you, can improve mental wellbeing.

The other three steps are connecting with other people, being physically active, and giving to others. A puzzle game can't do those for you, and it shouldn't replace them.

So the honest summary is: learning something new and challenging, and giving something your full attention, are both mentioned by the NHS as things that can help wellbeing. A puzzle *can* be one way of doing that. It is one small option among many, not a cure for anything.

## What the evidence doesn't say

You may have seen claims that puzzle games prevent dementia, raise IQ, or sharpen memory in everyday life. Be careful with them. Getting better at a game mostly makes you better at that game. Whether the skill carries over to other parts of life is much less clear, and scientists still disagree about it. We think the fair thing is simply not to make those claims, so we don't.

## How PaneSlide is designed to be calm

We have made design choices that put calm ahead of "engagement at any cost":

- **No timers.** You can think about a board for as long as you like.
- **No lives and no failing.** Every solve earns at least one star, and three stars come at par + 1, so one slip doesn't spoil a good solve.
- **One daily puzzle**, not an endless feed. When it's done, it's done, and you can put the phone down. Levels are there if you want more.
- **No money for progress.** Shards can only be earned by playing. Nothing in the game can be bought.
- **Gentle sound.** The music is soft and optional, with a few calm moods. Music, sound effects and vibration ("Buzz") each have their own switch in Settings.
- **Reduced motion.** If your device is set to reduce motion, PaneSlide switches off its interface animations and the shimmer on the glass.
- **No account and no tracking of your play.** Your progress stays on your device (see the [privacy policy](/privacy/)).

## Making puzzles a good habit

A few ideas that follow the spirit of the NHS steps, rather than any claim about the game itself:

### Keep it short

A puzzle is a good *break*, not a replacement for sleep, exercise or people. One daily, or a handful of levels, is plenty.

### Notice when you're stuck

Frustration is normal in puzzles, and it is a sign to pause rather than push. The guide to [getting unstuck](/guides/undo-hints-stuck/) suggests a simple routine: stop swiping, re-read the picture, and come back later if needed.

### Share it

The daily puzzle has a spoiler-free Share button. Sending your result to a friend or family group turns a solo puzzle into a small daily hello. Connecting with others is one of the NHS's five steps, and a shared puzzle is an easy reason to do it.

### Stop before bed if it keeps you up

Bright screens late at night aren't ideal for everyone. If a puzzle keeps your mind racing, try playing earlier in the day.

## If puzzles stop being fun

Games should add to your day. If you find you're playing far more than you want to, or it's affecting sleep, work or relationships, take a break, and talk to someone you trust or a professional if you're worried. In the UK, the NHS website lists mental health services; in India, the government's free, 24-hour Tele-MANAS helpline is 14416 (or 1-800-891-4416).
