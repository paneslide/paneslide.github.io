PaneSlide was designed for touch screens, but it plays very well on a laptop or desktop. On a big screen the layout spreads out, the board grows, and you can play entirely from the keyboard. This guide lists every control.

## Keyboard shortcuts

| Key | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Tab** or **1** to **9** | Pick a pane. Number keys pick panes in order; Tab moves between panes and buttons |
| **← ↑ → ↓** | Slide the picked pane |
| **W A S D** | Slide the picked pane, too |
| **Enter** | Pick the next pane that can move |
| **Z**, or **Ctrl + Z** (**⌘ + Z** on Mac) | Undo the last swipe |
| **H** | Use a hint (in Levels mode) |
| **M** | Turn music on or off |
| **Esc** | Close an open window, such as Settings or Stats |

A few details are worth knowing:

- **Arrows with nothing picked.** If no pane is picked yet, pressing an arrow picks the first pane that can slide that way. Press the arrow again to slide it.
- **Locked glass is skipped** when you use Enter to move between panes, because it can never move.
- **A ↔ pane only moves with ← and →**, and a ↕ pane only with ↑ and ↓, exactly as with swipes.
- **Shortcuts pause while a window is open** or while you are typing, for example in the progress code box, so you will not move glass by accident.
- **Undo still counts.** Pressing Z undoes a swipe but does not give it back, just like the undo button.

The game shows a small **Controls** panel beside the board on wide screens, so you do not need to remember them all.

## Using the mouse

- **Drag a pane** in the direction you want it to go. As with a finger swipe, the length of the drag does not matter: the pane glides until something stops it.
- **Hover** over a pane to highlight its edge. Locked glass does not highlight, which is a quick way to see what can move.
- The cursor changes to a hand over panes and a grabbing hand while you drag.
- **Buttons** respond to hover with a slight glow, and all of them work with a single click.

Trackpads work the same way: press, drag and release.

## The desktop layout

On a wide screen (roughly a laptop or larger, held landscape), PaneSlide switches to a layout made for it:

- **Home screen.** The title and daily information sit on the left, with the main buttons in a card on the right.
- **Playing a level.** The target picture and move meter sit on the left, a large board in the centre, and the Controls panel on the right. The board is much bigger than on a phone, which makes busy 5×5 levels easier to read.
- **Guides.** Articles use a comfortable reading width with a side panel listing the sections of the page and other guides in the same group.

On a tablet in portrait, or a small window, PaneSlide uses the familiar single-column layout, sized up to fit. Phones are unchanged.

## Tips for keyboard players

1. **Plan, then type.** Because keyboard swipes are precise and quick, it is easy to make several in a row. Keep the [plan-then-swipe habit](/guides/plan-your-swipes/) so you do not waste swipes.
2. **Use number keys on bigger boards.** On a level with five or six panes, number keys get you to the right pane in one press.
3. **Use H sparingly.** A hint shows the next best swipe. It never costs stars in Levels mode, but solving it yourself is more satisfying.
4. **Press M** if you are playing somewhere quiet. Sound effects have their own switch in Settings.

## Browser and zoom

PaneSlide works in current versions of Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari. If the board looks too small or too large, browser zoom (**Ctrl** with **+** or **−**, or **⌘** on Mac) works normally, and the layout adjusts.

For accessibility features such as Patterns and screen reader labels, see the [accessibility guide](/guides/accessibility/).
